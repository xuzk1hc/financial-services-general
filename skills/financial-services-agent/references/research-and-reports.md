# Research And Reports

Use this reference for equity research, earnings analysis, earnings previews, initiations, sector reports, thesis tracking, catalysts, screens, and market research.

## Common Research Contract

1. Verify the current date and the latest available source date.
2. Identify the decision question: what changed, what matters, what is mispriced, what to monitor, or what to compare.
3. Collect primary sources first: filings, earnings releases, transcripts, investor presentations, guidance, market data, and consensus where available.
4. Build tables and charts before writing conclusions.
5. Separate facts, calculations, management commentary, consensus, and agent interpretation.
6. Cite every table, chart, and key claim.

## Earnings Analysis

Use for post-earnings updates, quarterly result analysis, or model update notes.

Expected focus:

- Beat/miss versus consensus and guidance.
- Revenue, EPS, EBITDA/operating income, margins, FCF, and segment metrics.
- Why results differed from expectations.
- Guidance changes and estimate revisions.
- Thesis impact, catalysts, risks, and valuation implications.

Recommended output:

- Short executive summary.
- Beat/miss table.
- Segment or KPI table.
- 8-12 charts for full reports; fewer for quick notes.
- Updated estimates table.
- Source section.

Recency rule:

- Do not rely on memory for latest earnings.
- Verify release date, filing date, transcript date, and presentation date.
- If the latest quarter is unavailable, state the latest verified period.

## Earnings Preview

Use before a scheduled earnings event.

Include:

- Street expectations and management guidance.
- Key KPIs to watch.
- Scenario table: downside/base/upside.
- Debate map: what bulls and bears expect.
- Likely stock-moving items.
- Post-call checklist.

Do not present a prediction as certainty.

## Initiating Coverage

Use for a new full company report.

Core structure:

- Investment thesis.
- Company and business model.
- Industry and TAM.
- Competitive positioning.
- Financial model summary.
- Valuation framework.
- Risks.
- Catalysts.
- Appendix with detailed financials and sources.

Use more background than an earnings update. Include charts and peer comparisons for each major claim.

## Sector Overview

Use for industry landscape, thematic research, and market maps.

Core structure:

- Sector definition and value chain.
- Demand drivers and cycle position.
- Competitive landscape and share shifts.
- Unit economics and margin structure.
- Public and private company map.
- Regulatory, technology, and macro drivers.
- Peer comp table and valuation ranges.
- Investable ideas or watchlist, framed as research leads rather than recommendations.

## Thesis Tracker

Use when maintaining or updating an investment thesis.

Track:

- Thesis pillars.
- Evidence supporting/refuting each pillar.
- KPIs and source cadence.
- Catalysts and signposts.
- Variant perception.
- What would change the view.

## Catalyst Calendar

Use for upcoming events across coverage.

Include:

- Event date or expected window.
- Source and confidence.
- Expected metric/topic.
- Potential thesis impact.
- Required follow-up action.

## Idea Generation And Screens

Use for stock screens, theme discovery, or peer universe creation.

Define:

- Inclusion and exclusion rules.
- Geography, market cap, liquidity, sector, and data availability constraints.
- Core metrics and thresholds.
- Why each result belongs.
- Borderline names and why they were excluded or watchlisted.

Preserve the user's inclusion rule literally when it matters.

## Writing Standards

- Lead with the answer, then evidence.
- Use tables for comparisons and charts for time series.
- Avoid generic business commentary.
- Distinguish "reported", "adjusted", "consensus", "guidance", and "estimate".
- Put source notes close to exhibits.
- Keep a final section for risks, open questions, and data gaps.
